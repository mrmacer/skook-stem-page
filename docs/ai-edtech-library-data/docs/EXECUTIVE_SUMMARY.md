# Executive Summary — EdTech & AI Resource Library V1

**77 records · 15 collections · 9 documented exclusions · compiled July 24, 2026**

## Strongest source organizations

Common Sense (digital citizenship, AI lessons, privacy program, AI ratings — four distinct high-value assets), the U.S. Department of Education OET stack (2023 report → 2024 toolkit → 2025 funding letter), TeachAI (the most adaptable policy toolkit), AI4K12 (the conceptual framework everything else should align to), MIT RAISE (Day of AI, DAILy), and CAST (UDL 3.0, AEM Center). In Pennsylvania: PDE's new Implementing AI in Schools guidance, PaTTAN, and the SDPC PA alliance.

## Strongest classroom-ready collections

Common Sense AI Literacy Lessons, Day of AI, Experience AI, and Code.org AI — all free, all deliverable with minimal prep, most with zero student accounts. "Ready for Monday Morning" is a real collection here, not marketing.

## Strongest district governance sources

PDE guidance + the federal toolkit + TeachAI toolkit is the policy triad; the CoSN/CGCS readiness checklist operationalizes it; the AI for Education state tracker benchmarks it. That is a complete policy workflow for a district starting from zero.

## Best privacy resources

Common Sense Privacy Program (independent evaluations), SDPC registry (find an existing DPA before signing a new one), Student Privacy Compass, PTAC, and FTC COPPA guidance. Together they let a district verify instead of trusting "built for schools" claims — the exact posture the site philosophy demands.

## Areas dominated by vendor marketing

Teacher productivity platforms, above all. Every claim of hours saved is vendor-surveyed. The frontier-lab educator programs (ChatGPT for Teachers, Claude for Teachers, Gemini for Education) are strategic land-grabs with free-until dates; the library records them accurately, including the pricing cliffs.

## Areas with weak evidence

Learning-outcome evidence for nearly every generative AI tool. ASSISTments stands nearly alone with RCT-grade evidence. Khanmigo's own transition acknowledges engagement was harder than hyped. The library says so in the evidenceQuality field rather than pretending otherwise.

## Major gaps in the external landscape

(1) Elementary-appropriate AI literacy remains thin below grade 3. (2) AI + special education is badly underserved (CIDDL is the best of a small field) — which is precisely why the Living IEP work matters. (3) Family-facing AI resources are shallow relative to demand. (4) Pennsylvania-specific classroom materials essentially do not exist yet — the STEELS-aligned original-content opportunity is wide open.

## Recommended first public release

The 14 live-verified records plus the high-confidence catalog-review records (Common Sense, AI4K12, CAST, federal sources, Teachable Machine, Scratch, Code.org, Day of AI, Experience AI, Checkology, COR, PTAC, FTC, SDPC, PaTTAN, Khanmigo) — roughly 40 records — with the remaining medium-confidence records released as each clears first live verification. Ship with the "Start Here," "Monday Morning," "Policy Starter," and "PA Connections" collections.

## Should remain district-only

The AI AUP template (solicitor review before public release), procurement question banks, and any incident-response guidance. Public release of half-finished policy artifacts creates liability for member districts.

## Tools requiring immediate local review before any student-facing use

Gemini for Education / NotebookLM (now ON by default at the top OU in many Workspace configurations — districts must make an active decision), MagicSchool student Rooms, SchoolAI Spaces, and Microsoft Copilot student access (least stable facts in the catalog).

## Most time-sensitive information

The three educator-program pricing cliffs (OpenAI June 2027, Anthropic free-year window, Google add-on tiers), all freemium tier limits, PDE guidance expansion and any PA legislative movement, and FTC COPPA amendment requirements. All flagged in STATS.md for quarterly re-verification.
