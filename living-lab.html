Schuylkill Living Lab

Our community is the classroom.

Students across Schuylkill County observe, document, investigate, and improve the environments around their schools and communities — turning their own backyards into a regional science laboratory.

Explore the Living Lab Submit an Observation Teacher Toolkit
How it works

Explore → Document → Investigate → Act

Every classroom starts the same way: step outside, notice something, and write it down. From there, Living Lab gives students a path from a single observation to a real regional investigation — and eventually, to doing something about what they find.

Apps like Merlin and Seek are useful tools along the way, but they're not the point. The science belongs to the students — technology just helps them see it clearly.

K–12 progression

Participation grows with every grade band

Living Lab isn't one assignment — it's a throughline from kindergarten noticing to senior-year policy work.

K–2
What lives here?
  • Noticing & observing
  • Listening & drawing
  • Identifying & comparing seasons
Teacher-managed technology
3–5
What patterns do we see?
  • Repeated observations & counts
  • Basic biodiversity
  • Habitat comparisons & displaying data
Structured student contributions begin
6–8
Why are these patterns happening?
  • Environmental variables
  • Comparing sites & land use
  • Seasonal patterns & PA issues
Data analysis begins
9–12
What should we do about it?
  • Monitoring, GIS & statistics
  • Bioindicators & engineering
  • Public communication & policy
